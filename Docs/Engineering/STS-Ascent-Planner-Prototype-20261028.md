# Ascent planner prototype and replay check

The default launch executive uses an altitude-based turn and an apoapsis corridor before booster separation. It now also evaluates two-stage candidate forecasts every 10 simulation seconds after the initial roll settles. For the standard orbiter with solid boosters, a candidate's pitch bias is used only if the approximate model predicts an acceptable orbit; otherwise the existing turn continues. The insertion optimizer is independently initialized after separation. Each phase emits separately labeled forecast events.

A rough replay seeded near UT 155317.6 in `runs/20261028T031525Z-launch/ascent.csv` illustrates why the model is not yet suitable for flight control. The sample had altitude 2.17 km, radial speed 138 m/s, surface horizontal speed 58 m/s, mass 407.8 t, measured SRB thrust 5.51 MN, and approximately 57.4 s of booster burn remaining. The prototype, using approximate rotational speed, an estimated 0.8 MN core thrust, 330 kg/s core and 2340 kg/s booster mass flow, 41 t discarded mass and 2 m/s² initial drag, predicted separation at 13.2 km with radial speed 200 m/s and mass 213.4 t. The recorded sample immediately after separation was at 19.9 km, 547 m/s and 182.0 t. These numbers are not a validation of any particular source of error: the model inputs include estimates, not recorded measurements of each quantity.

Each replan now seeds current engine thrust and flow, mass, drag, lift and measured thrust direction. The extrapolation still assumes constant future thrust and mass flow, a simple atmosphere, constant rotation offset, and first-order pitch response; it also calls the existing insertion predictor, which does not model the observed large attitude tracking errors. It does not model the full initial roll and launch hold. The new closed loop has not been validated in a launch. Use its phase-labeled telemetry to compare future forecasts with actual separation and MECO, and independently validate the attitude response and aerodynamic model. A successful point-mass test alone does not establish flight readiness.
