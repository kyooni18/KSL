# Ascent planner prototype and replay check

The default launch executive currently uses an altitude-based turn and an apoapsis corridor before booster separation. Its insertion optimizer starts after separation. `ksl/ascent/full_plan.py` is an offline, experimental two-stage candidate forecast; it is deliberately not connected to the launch executive.

A rough replay seeded near UT 155317.6 in `runs/20261028T031525Z-launch/ascent.csv` illustrates why the model is not yet suitable for flight control. The sample had altitude 2.17 km, radial speed 138 m/s, surface horizontal speed 58 m/s, mass 407.8 t, measured SRB thrust 5.51 MN, and approximately 57.4 s of booster burn remaining. The prototype, using approximate rotational speed, an estimated 0.8 MN core thrust, 330 kg/s core and 2340 kg/s booster mass flow, 41 t discarded mass and 2 m/s² initial drag, predicted separation at 13.2 km with radial speed 200 m/s and mass 213.4 t. The recorded sample immediately after separation was at 19.9 km, 547 m/s and 182.0 t. These numbers are not a validation of any particular source of error: the model inputs include estimates, not recorded measurements of each quantity.

The extrapolation assumes constant thrust and mass flow, a simple atmosphere, constant rotation offset, and first-order pitch response; it also calls the existing insertion predictor, which does not model the observed large attitude tracking errors. It does not model the full initial roll and launch hold. Before commanding flight, collect synchronized inertial velocity, thrust vector, aerodynamic force, propellant flow, stage mass, and attitude/rate measurements; validate full-horizon predictions against recorded flights and an independent simulator, including separation and MECO. Replanning must honor actual roll/separation holds, load limits, gimbal authority and the user's manual-only abort policy. A successful point-mass test alone does not establish flight readiness.
