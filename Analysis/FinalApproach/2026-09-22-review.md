# Final approach review — 2026-09-22

Scope: current modular `CLanding/`, with existing migration/HAC work preserved.
No live KSP connection, launch, or flight was used. The changes improve response
accounting and stage transitions; they do not establish a successful autoland.

## Current behavior

Final guidance progresses through trajectory capture, outer final, preflare,
inner final, touchdown flare, and ground rollout. Progression is monotonic.
Gear confirmation, runway geometry, speed, dynamic pressure, load, and vertical
recovery checks constrain advancement. Atmospheric thrust and airbrakes remain
disabled. Ground rollout uses a separate controller.

The current configuration specifies a 3,200 m outer-final station, 20 degree
glide slope (about 1,165 m above the runway there), 160 m/s alignment speed,
55 m nominal flare altitude, and -1.5 m/s touchdown sink target.

## Corrected defects

1. **Recovery timing ignored the guidance reference.** The vehicle's reported
   pitch capability could be faster than the AoA reference actually issued by
   `stabilized()`. Preflare height and energy accounting could therefore assume
   lift arrived before it could be commanded. Guidance and prediction now share
   the same reference rate/acceleration limits. Final recovery timing includes
   aircraft state and any existing pitch/roll limiter state, including a
   reference moving away from the recovery target. Missing physical authority
   still invalidates the envelope. Preflare forecasts use the final response
   limits even before final capture, avoiding a timing change at handoff.
2. **A ballooning pull-up could count as shallow-glide capture.** The old
   one-sided sink comparison admitted arbitrarily positive vertical speed.
   Preflare now requires absolute sink error within the existing 2 m/s band
   throughout the existing confirmation interval.
3. **A valid sample could trigger the invalid-corridor timeout.** At zero elapsed
   time and zero response time, the old comparison accepted `0 >= 0` as an abort.
   Expiration now aborts only while the current corridor is invalid. A recovered
   corridor likewise cannot abort solely because its old timer is still decaying.

The attitude-limit extraction preserves the existing executable slew settings.
These changes do not tune aerodynamic tables or relax physical limits.

## Offline verification

All six checked suites passed: approach sequence, terminal recovery, attitude
slew, decision envelopes, guidance integration, and TAEM production integration.
The new tests cover climbing preflare, successful subsequent sink capture,
zero-time valid telemetry, slower configured guidance, limiter reversal,
missing pitch authority, and forecast/execution timing agreement.

The response regression advances the production attitude limiter at 100 Hz
with a perfectly following plant:

| Case | Response time |
| --- | ---: |
| Aircraft capability alone, representative fixture | 3.850 s |
| Same state with executable default guidance limits | 6.500 s |
| Deliberately slower guidance limits, forecast | 26.500 s |
| Slower limits, simulated delivery within 0.01 degree | 26.130 s |

This checks command delivery, not aerodynamic touchdown performance.

A new transport-free gate runs the three focused suites without building the
backend or linking its SQLite/kRPC components:

```sh
make -C CLanding final-approach-test
```

In this Linux environment the exact successful invocation was:

```sh
make -C CLanding BUILD=/tmp/shuttle-final-native \
  CFLAGS='-O2 -std=gnu17 -Wall -Wextra -pthread' final-approach-test
```

The existing build artifacts were for another platform. Full `qualification`
could not finish a fresh native build because `sqlite3.h` is unavailable.
GCC also reports pre-existing warnings that the default `-Werror` promotes;
the local invocation above keeps warnings enabled without making them fatal.
The three wider suites were compiled against the same offline core objects
and run directly. No repository compiler defaults were weakened.

## Simulator findings and remaining work

A temporary in-process probe loaded the existing fitted atmosphere, robust
aerodynamic table, direct-force book, attitude parameters, and current
configuration. It decoded telemetry through the production simulator adapter
and requested a production preflare plan before permitting further simulation.
At 10 degrees initial AoA, wings level, runway heading, and zero cross-track,
these independent downstream initial states all failed the projected preflare
speed-reserve gate, both before and after the corrections:

| Radar height | Airspeed | Flight-path angle | Along-track |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1,165 m | 160 m/s | -20 degrees | -3,200 m |
| 300 m | 140 m/s | -5 degrees | -1,000 m |
| 50 m | 110 m/s | -2 degrees | -200 m |

These are rejected initial-state screens, **not completed simulator flights**.
They do not show that every possible final approach is infeasible, and no
landing-success rate is claimed. Scratch probe source and driver for this
session are `/tmp/FinalApproachProbe.c` and `/tmp/final_probe.py`.

Further issues remain visible in the current implementation:

- The preflare height model assumes maximum recoverable lift after the response
  interval, while execution blends desired sink with altitude. Full trajectory
  validation is still needed to establish that execution delivers the assumed
  vertical acceleration and retains energy throughout the pull-up.
- The preflare energy estimate charges maximum-target drag across its entire
  response/recovery duration. The rejected screens above warrant checking this
  approximation against integrated trajectories before changing either the
  energy gate or the control law.
- The default 75 m/s touchdown target lies below the 85 m/s airborne minimum
  speed. This needs an explicit, supported landing-speed contract; lowering
  the existing safety floor without evidence would not resolve it.
- The current simulator's attitude step follows guidance commands directly.
  These checks do not validate live actuator lag, ground effect, contact
  dynamics, or an end-to-end HAC-to-stopped-runway landing.

The revised timing can reject approaches that the old timing admitted. That is
an intentional correction of optimistic feasibility, not evidence of improved
touchdown performance. The next useful simulator task is a physically feasible
final-delivery fixture with recorded force, energy, sink, and command histories
through every downstream stage.
